## What to Consider When Buying a Budget Smartphone

### Display Quality

A good display makes everything better. Look for AMOLED or OLED panels with at least 90Hz refresh rate for smooth scrolling. Screen brightness is important if you use your phone outdoors frequently – aim for at least 1000 nits peak brightness.

### Camera Performance

Megapixels aren't everything. Look for phones with larger sensors, optical image stabilization (OIS), and good low-light performance. Check sample photos and reviews to see real-world camera quality rather than relying on spec sheets alone.

### Battery Life and Charging

A 5,000mAh battery is becoming standard in budget phones. Fast charging (at least 30W) is increasingly common and makes a huge difference in daily use. Consider whether wireless charging is important to you, as it's still rare in budget devices.

### Software Updates

Long-term software support is crucial for security and getting new features. Google and Samsung lead with 5+ years of updates, but most manufacturers now offer at least 2-3 years of major Android updates.

### Build Quality and Design

Water resistance (IP67 or IP68) provides valuable protection. Consider whether you prefer curved or flat displays, and check if the phone has a headphone jack if that matters to you.
